HOW TO PROTECT KIDS ONLINE

Kids need rules, tools, and a grown-up they can tell. All three matter.

Daily article — November 5, 2026  |  Silent Security Vault — Domenick Arlon Hall, Mobile, AL

The internet is useful for kids and dangerous for kids at the same time. The goal is not to scare them — it is to give them rules they understand, tools that help, and a grown-up they can tell when something feels wrong.

What you need to know

  • Talk first, tools second. Tell your child: never share your name, school, address, or photos with people online, and tell me if someone asks.
  • Keep screens in shared rooms. A tablet in the living room is safer than a phone alone in a bedroom late at night.
  • Use the built-in controls. Phones and tablets have parental controls for time, apps, and content. Turn them on together, so your child knows the rules.
  • Check the friends list. Know who your child talks to online. A 'friend' who is really an adult is the danger to watch for.
  • Make telling safe. If your child tells you about a scary message, thank them first. A child who is punished for telling will stop telling.

What to do next

Sit with your child this week and go through their apps together. Five minutes of looking beats a year of guessing.
